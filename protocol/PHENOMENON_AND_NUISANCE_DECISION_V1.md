## 1. Operational definition

For the current repository claim, define the target phenomenon provisionally as:

> **Excess spectral persistence:** a higher value of the prespecified canonical spectral exponent (`canonical_primary_alpha`) in the observed primary series than expected under an adequate, prospectively specified stochastic null representing the declared non-target temporal structure.

The test direction is upper-tail (`observed alpha > null`), consistent with `core-scientific/strict_claim.json`. This is an operational statistical target; it does not establish HCM causation, consciousness, a universal mechanism, or a new physical law.

This definition treats the current claim as **excess persistence beyond an explicit baseline**, not merely the discovery of long-memory by choosing a null that removes the target by construction. If the intended scientific claim is instead that long-memory itself is the target, the estimand and null must be reconsidered before confirmation.

## 2. Null and alternative

- **H0:** Given the declared sampling process and source-specific nuisance structure, the observed canonical alpha is not unusually high relative to the distribution generated by the prespecified null family and its full fitting/refitting procedure.
- **H1:** The observed canonical alpha is greater than the upper-tail reference distribution under that adequate null.
- **Primary endpoint:** `canonical_primary_alpha` only. Estimator, normalization, band, transformation, window, exclusions, direction/tail, surrogate generation, and decision rule must be fixed before confirmation.
- **Monte Carlo p-value:** retain `(exceedances + 1) / (valid_surrogates + 1)` unless an independent methods review approves a different rule prospectively.

The recorded current p-value `0.999001` is not a rejection of the current exploratory null. It does not establish that all stochastic explanations have been excluded and does not falsify HCM as a whole.

## 3. Nuisance structure requiring explicit treatment

The protocol must justify treatment of:

1. Sampling cadence, timestamps, gaps, missing values, source revisions, and measurement error.
2. Trend, level shifts, regime changes, and other nonstationarity.
3. Seasonality and known periodic components.
4. Short-lag autocorrelation and spectral structure outside or adjacent to the target band.
5. Marginal distribution, heavy tails, outliers, and non-Gaussian innovations.
6. Conditional heteroskedasticity or time-varying variance.
7. Possible long-memory dependence and uncertainty in estimating it.
8. Domain-specific drivers and source-vintage changes.
9. Estimator, model-selection, and finite-sample uncertainty.

No one null family is assumed to preserve every feature exactly. Adequacy is relative to the stated H0, estimand, data-generating assumptions, and calibrated decision rule—not to whichever model gives a favorable p-value.

## 4. Target-versus-nuisance boundary

For each feature, the methods record must classify it as target, nuisance preserved under H0, nuisance modeled/conditioned on, measurement defect/exclusion, or unresolved. Long-memory cannot be both the defining target and silently removed as nuisance without changing the scientific question. Conversely, if the target is excess persistence beyond ordinary short-memory dynamics, the null must represent the relevant short-memory structure adequately without being chosen from candidate endpoint values or p-values.
