# Future Confirmatory Null Protocol

## Status

PROSPECTIVE METHODOLOGICAL PROTOCOL

This document defines requirements for any future confirmatory validation of the Constrained Spectral Persistence Hypothesis.

It is not evidence for the hypothesis.

The current canonical result remains under investigation.

---

## Current Null Limitation

The current exploratory primary stochastic null uses a stationary Gaussian AR(p) family with AIC order selection over p = 1..20.

In the current canonical run, 96.5% of surrogate fits select the maximum declared order.

The associated capacity audit also shows boundary saturation when the explored order cap is increased.

Therefore the current AR(1..20) specification is not treated as a fully calibrated confirmatory null.

No post-observation increase of the AR order range is permitted for the purpose of obtaining a preferred scientific result.

---

## Requirements Before Fresh Confirmation

Before any fresh confirmation dataset is evaluated, the following must be frozen:

1. Primary estimator.
2. Frequency band.
3. Segmentation and windowing parameters.
4. Primary endpoint.
5. Direction of the alternative.
6. Tail of the statistical test.
7. Null-family candidate set.
8. Null-family selection rule.
9. Parameter-fitting rule.
10. Surrogate-generation rule.
11. Number of surrogate trials.
12. Monte Carlo p-value rule.
13. Domain eligibility rules.
14. Replication criteria.
15. Multiple-testing policy.
16. Falsification criteria.

---

## Null-Family Selection

If multiple stochastic null families are considered, the selection rule must be declared before the confirmation result is inspected.

Model selection may use an explicitly designated training, calibration, or reference dataset.

The confirmation dataset must remain untouched by null-family selection.

The final selected null specification must be frozen before the confirmation endpoint is evaluated.

---

## No Post-Observation Rescue

The following are prohibited after observing the confirmation result:

- changing the null family to obtain rejection;
- changing the AR order range to obtain rejection;
- switching from an upper-tail to a lower-tail test;
- changing the primary endpoint;
- changing the frequency band;
- changing the estimator;
- excluding inconvenient observations;
- changing eligibility rules;
- promoting a diagnostic null to primary status;
- replacing an unsuccessful test with a more favorable test.

Such analyses may be retained as exploratory analyses, but they cannot retroactively become confirmatory evidence.

---

## Fresh Confirmation Data

A confirmatory result must be evaluated on data that were not used to select the final null specification or estimator configuration.

The confirmation dataset must have its provenance recorded before evaluation.

--+

## Replication

Independent real-domain replication requires:

- the same endpoint;
- the same null family;
- the same direction;
- the same tail;
- the same inferential rule;
- independently eligible real domains;
- domain-level rejection of the declared primary null.

Measurement validity alone is not replication.

Derived, shuffled, synthetic, or null-generated datasets do not count as independent real-domain replication.

---

## Interpretation

Rejection of a declared stochastic null would provide evidence against that null under the stated protocol.

It would not by itself establish:

- HCM causation;
- consciousness;
- universality;
- a physical field;
- a novel physical law;
- mechanism;
- market advantage.

Failure to reject remains a valid scientific outcome.

---

## Governing Principle

The purpose of the confirmatory protocol is not to maximize the probability of obtaining a positive result.

The purpose is to make the result interpretable before the result is known.
